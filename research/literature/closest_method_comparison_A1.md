## Crowd guide optimisation

Von Schantz, Ehtamo and Hostikka, *Minimization of mean-CVaR evacuation time of a crowd using rescue guides*, author version 2010.03922v2, §§2–4. It couples social-force crowd/guide behaviour with probabilistic scenarios, evacuation-time mean/tail objectives and simulation-based genetic optimisation. Uncertainty concerns destinations and desired speeds. Our one-epoch zone-capacity planner has a different loss and information-channel comparison, but uncertain crowd resource optimisation is established. Do not claim a first probabilistic evacuation planner. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.03922v2)

## Resource planning under uncertain demand

Moug, Jia and Shen, *A Shared Mobility Based Framework for Evacuation Planning and Operations under Forecast Uncertainty*, author preprint, §2. It uses a spatial/temporal transport network, pre-event recruitment and later routing/assignment under demand uncertainty, with expectation-based and chance-constrained formulations. Our static pedestrian-zone shortfall model is substantially simpler; classification-probability versus hard-label controls are our benchmark focus. This does not establish new stochastic-programming machinery. The accessible preprint must not be described as a line-by-line audit of the later journal version. [Primary preprint](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/7963.pdf)

## Bayesian crowd calibration

Gödel, Bode, Köster and Bungartz, *Bayesian inference methods to calibrate crowd dynamics models for safety applications*, Safety Science 147, 105586, publisher PDF via Bristol. The work compares point and Bayesian parameter calibration using bottleneck scenarios; posterior shape can expose uncertainty that a point estimate omits. Our uncertain occupant/service-profile metadata is a different latent variable, not a new Bayes rule. This source also cautions against pretending a fitted simulator parameter is certain. [Primary publisher PDF](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/312771599/1_s2.0_S0925753521004276_main.pdf)

## Recognition-plus-time predecessor

The earlier Stage 1 inspection of Lin et al., arXiv:2201.05877, found LiDAR size/movement subclassification and arrival-time models, with rule-derived labels (§2.1.3). Recognition plus prediction is therefore not a sufficient novelty claim. Our present implementation does not perform independent demographic recognition; it explicitly models a hypothetical noisy metadata channel. Preserve this limitation rather than comparing synthetic probabilities with published recognition accuracy.

## Positioning decision

An exploratory **information-to-decision benchmark** is defensible: conditional metadata probabilities → expected zone composition → exact capacity allocation, with matched hard-label/population/mean-demand controls and misspecification stress tests. The optimisation ingredients and conditional-probability algebra are established methods. Real trajectories support occupancy integration; resource demand and recognition inputs remain hypothetical. No full general-purpose simulator or first mobility-aware framework claim is supported.

The remaining research value depends on the transparent benchmark, its conditional/negative findings and a useful explanation of when information changes decisions. Course acceptance and indexed publication are not established. Further broader citation tracing or university database access may reveal closer work; report this coverage limit in the paper rather than asserting absence.
